# Introduction
French fries are a popular food item enjoyed globally, often served as a side dish or snack. The purpose of this report is to provide an in-depth examination of French fries, covering their definition, history, types, preparation methods, nutritional value, cultural significance, and future developments.

## Definition and Classification
French fries are defined as dishes of fried elongated pieces of potatoes. The term "French fries" is commonly used in the United States and most of Canada, while other variants, such as "pommes frites," are used in other regions. According to [Source 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_fries), the term "French fries" refers to all dishes of fried elongated pieces of potatoes, with variations in shape and size having names such as curly fries or shoe-string fries.

## History and Origin
The origin of French fries is often attributed to Belgium, with [Source 2](https://www.billyparisi.com/perfect-pommes-frites-recipe-french-fries) stating that the Belgians are the creators and founders of pommes frites, which translates to fried potatoes. This challenges the common assumption that French fries originated in France.

## Types and Varieties
French fries come in various shapes, sizes, and flavors. Some common types include curly fries, shoe-string fries, and thick-cut fries. ## Preparation Methods
The conventional method of making French fries involves soaking raw fries in water, followed by a double fry. This method is still widely used by many restaurants and pubs, as mentioned in [Source 4](https://www.recipetineats.com/french-fries). Other recipes, such as [Source 5](https://www.seriouseats.com/perfect-french-fries-recipe), provide alternative methods for achieving perfect thin and crispy French fries.
